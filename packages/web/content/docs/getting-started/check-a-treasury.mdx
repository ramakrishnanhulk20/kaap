Open [Check a treasury](/check), type the account of any Sputnik DAO on NEAR, and press **Read it**. You need no wallet and no sign-in.

## What you get

- **What it says.** The description, as the treasury's members see it.
- **What it will do.** Each effect in one plain sentence, with the exact fields one click away.
- **What Kaap found.** The findings, each with a severity.
- **The risk**, from low to critical, and whether the guard would veto.
- **How it was read.** The block, the two providers that agreed, the treasury's code, and where the payment history and prices came from.

Leave the request number empty for the latest request, or type one. **Earlier request** and **Later request** step through the rest.

## Two requests worth opening

- **A theft that was stopped.** [Request 1 on the demo treasury](/check?dao=kaap-dao-1111072928.testnet&network=testnet&id=1) said it paid alice and paid a lookalike account. The page shows Kaap's reading and the four transactions that ended in the guard's reject vote.
- **A request that shows only a hash.** [Request 7 on the demo treasury](/check?dao=kaap-dao-1111072928.testnet&network=testnet&id=7) reads as high risk and unreadable. Press **Use the words saved for this demo request** and Kaap checks that the words hash to the same value, reads them, and the risk becomes critical: the request adds a key to the treasury's funds.

## File a fake theft yourself

Under the check is a button: **File the fake theft**. One press files a real request on the demo treasury, on NEAR testnet. It says it pays alice and it pays a lookalike account. Nobody tells Kaap. Its watcher has to notice the request, have it read inside sealed hardware and get the guard to reject it, and the page shows each step as it lands, with a link to every transaction.

The page files with a key that the network itself limits to one action: filing a request on that one treasury. It cannot vote, pay or reach any other contract, and anyone can check that on the explorer. The demo runs at most 40 times a day.

The button needs Kaap's watcher to be running. Where it is not, the page says so, and the recorded stopped theft above works at any time.

## What this check is and is not

It is the plain-code part of Kaap, run on our server with the same code as the [command line](/docs/getting-started/command-line). It reads the chain at the newest final block from two providers, and it gives no answer if they disagree.

It is not a verdict. The second opinion from the AI and the guard's vote happen only in Kaap's sealed program, for treasuries that have enrolled. The page says so under every reading.

Checks are limited to a few a minute for each visitor, so the public providers are not flooded.
