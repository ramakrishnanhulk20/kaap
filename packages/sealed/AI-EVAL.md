# Kaap's AI second opinion, measured on the answer key

Generated by `node eval/tools/ai-report.mjs` from the rows in `eval/ai-runs/` and the labels in `eval/real/labels.jsonl` and `eval/planted/labels.jsonl`; every count below is its output, and `--check` proves the file is exactly what the rows give.

## What was run

Kaap's sealed job, build `d8453d6e674d15b1c8acd7796e4219f9388e5704cd8996b7f6642370574e9083`, on OutLayer testnet (project `kaap-worker-1029053612.testnet/kaap-sealed`), in eval mode: the job takes a labelled proposal as its input, runs the same plain-code analysis and the same NEAR AI call as a judge run, and prints the scores unsigned. The model is `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`, the one the job pins. Runs went from 2026-11-11 12:12 UTC to 2026-11-11 16:24 UTC. Each pass sent every record of its split as the job would see it, and sent a record again only after a run of failed calls (see the availability section). Nothing was tuned to the labels: no prompt, no rule and no setting changed between passes except the reasoning budget.

The AI is a second opinion, never the only proof. Only a verified answer may raise a verdict, and the guard vetoes only a critical verdict on a proposal where a veto is allowed, which needs a plain-code finding of high or critical (labelling guide, section 6).

## Why there is a split

A setting chosen by looking at the same records that then measure it would flatter the result. So the 373 records were split by a rule fixed before any full run: the development split is lines 1, 6, 11, ... (every fifth, counting from 1) of `eval/real/sample.jsonl` and of `eval/planted/planted.jsonl`, 75 records; the test split is the other 298. The 75 development records carry the three reasoning-budget passes and the choice between them; the test split ran once, at the chosen budget, and gives the headline numbers. The split files are `eval/ai-runs/split-development.txt` and `split-test.txt`, and this tool refuses them unless they follow the rule. An earlier probe, on the build before this one, sent only records that are now in the development split, so no test record had been sent before its pass.

The choice rule, fixed before these passes: take 512, the budget judge mode ships with, unless another budget has at least 3 more usable answers on the development split, or at least 2 more AI catches with no more false alarms; if two budgets both beat 512, take the smaller. Budget 256: -2 usable answers, +0 catches, +0 false alarms against 512. Budget 1024: -22 usable answers, +0 catches, +0 false alarms against 512. The rule picks 512.

Why budget 1024 lost answers: 24 of 75 of its calls ended `call_failed`, against 0 and 0 at 256 and 512. The likely cause, not a proven one: the job waits at most 25 s for the first byte of the chat answer (`CHAT_LIMITS` in `packages/sealed/src/ai.rs`), the request is not streamed, so nothing arrives until the model has finished thinking, and with more thinking the answer more often arrives later than that. A wait that runs out is reported as `call_failed`, the same word as a call the service refused, so the rows alone cannot tell the two apart. The worker times are the evidence there is: at budget 1024 the `call_failed` rows took 30.8 to 34.8 s and the usable answers 14.2 to 33.6 s. The pass also ran later in the day than the other two, during the same reported outage.

## Availability during the run

NEAR AI's status page (status.near.ai) reported a major outage of its chat service (its Chat API) at 11:59 UTC on 11 Nov 2026, before the first run, and still at 14:28 UTC. So one record was sent first as a check, at budget 512, to be resent every 15 minutes while it failed with `call_failed` or `gateway_unavailable`; it answered on the first try. During a pass, records went out in batches of up to 16; when a batch ended with five or more records in a row failing that way, the pass paused for 15 minutes and then sent those records again, and a record that failed three times stayed failed. That is the only resend rule: no record was sent again for any other reason, and nothing was re-run to get a cleaner number.

A platform failure is a call with no usable AI answer because a call did not complete or a check could not fetch what it needed (`call_failed`, `deadline`, `response_unreadable`, `catalog_unreadable`, `gateway_unavailable`, `report_unavailable`, `collateral_unavailable`, `gpu_unavailable`, `signature_unavailable`, `no_clock`), or because the OutLayer run or its transaction failed or returned nothing readable (`run_failed`, `tx_failed`, `unreadable_output`). An answer refused is one the model gave that the job's reader would not accept. Both count as no answer, and both are part of the result.

Every `unreadable_output` row in these passes (20 in all) is a request whose OutLayer run never completed: its transaction logs no completion event, returns `null` and refunds the deposit, which can be read on the explorer from the row's `tx`. Such a row has no worker time, and each took 111.2 to 132.0 s to settle in the test pass. It is a failure of OutLayer's run, not an answer from the job.

The test pass made 2 pauses, from 15:20 to 15:35 UTC after 16 records in a row ended `call_failed`, and from 15:41 to 15:56 UTC after 16 records in a row ended `call_failed`. 16 records were sent again under the rule, 32 tries in all; as the pass ended, 11 were `call_failed`, 4 were `unreadable_output`, 1 had a usable answer.

The bar fixed before the run for the test pass was at most 2 percent of its records failing for a platform reason. It was not met. The pass stopped under that rule at 14:27 UTC, after 160 rows, 6 of them `call_failed` (3.8%). It was then completed from 14:33 UTC with that stop lifted and everything else as before: the same resend rule, the same budget, no setting changed. As it ended, 93 of 298 test records (31.2%) had failed for a platform reason: 74 `call_failed`, 19 `unreadable_output`.

A further pass, over a private set of records kept outside this repository, ran after the test pass at the same budget and under the same rules; its results are not published here. It was interrupted once, when the machine running it stopped, and was resumed from its rows: records already sent were read back from their transactions, not sent again.

## Definitions

- Plain code vetoes: the label says `should_veto` and `must_veto`, so the floor is critical and the guard vetoes whatever the AI says. Counted: how many the AI also called critical.
- Only the AI could veto: `should_veto` is true, `must_veto` is false and `veto_allowed` is true. A catch is an answer that said critical and was verified; every other record here is a miss.
- False alarm: `veto_allowed` is true and `should_veto` is false, and the AI said critical and was verified.
- As the guard would act: a veto is a final risk of critical on a record where `veto_allowed` is true. With the AI on, the final risk is the job's own `risk`, which falls back to the plain-code floor when the AI gave no usable verified answer; with the AI off it is the plain-code `floor`. Caught and missed are out of the records that should be vetoed, wrongly vetoed out of those that should not. A record with no verdict, because its run never completed, is not vetoed either way.
- Known gap: `should_veto` is true but `veto_allowed` is false, the one kind the Guard role can never reject. A miss either way.
- AI risk against the floor: every usable answer's risk word compared with the job's floor.

## What this does not show

- One model, `z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`, on one day, under a reported outage of NEAR AI's chat service and with some OutLayer runs never completing. On another day the same records may get more answers.
- 298 test records. A difference of a few records between AI on and off is within what another sample could change.
- The records the AI answered may be the easier ones (see the headline), so its numbers where it answered may not hold on every record.
- The labels were made by AI labelling passes following the guide, and cross-checked on a sample by a blind second pass (`eval/README.md`). A wrong label moves a number here.
- No adversary adapted to Kaap's prompt. The planted attacks are fixed records; an attacker who can try descriptions against the same model may do better.
- Eval mode prints unsigned scores. A judge run adds the signature and the evidence record, which this run does not measure.
